Objective
The goal of this project is to formulate, implement, analyze, and communicate an optimization model for a meaningful decision problem.

The focus is not only on obtaining a solution, but also on making good modeling decisions, understanding the results, and being able to justify the complete work.

Teams and timeline
The project will be completed in groups of three students.

There will be two mandatory intermediate submissions. They are not graded. Their purpose is to receive feedback and identify potential problems early.

Project proposal – October 11
Submit a short proposal (maximum 2 pages) including: 1) problem and motivation, 2) main decisions and objective, 3) main constraints, 4) expected data sources, 5) main modeling challenges,  and 6) preliminary mathematical formulation.

Model checkpoint – November 9
Submit a draft report including: 1) available data, 2) preliminary model implementation, 3) initial results, and 4) problems or difficulties encountered. The model does not need to be complete at this stage.

Topic selection
The topic is open. You are encouraged to choose a problem related to your engineering discipline or to an area that interests you. Interdisciplinary projects are also welcome.

Possible areas include energy, manufacturing, logistics and transportation, scheduling, telecommunications, facility location, maintenance, inventory, resource allocation, and network optimization.

These are only examples. Your project must address a meaningful decision problem for which optimization can provide useful information.

Data
Each team is responsible for finding, collecting, or generating the data needed for the project. You may use real-world, open-source, or synthetic data. You must explain where the data come from and justify any important assumptions.

Mathematical formulation
The final report must include the complete mathematical formulation of the optimization problem:

Sets and indices

Parameters and input data

Decision variables

Objective function

Constraints

The equations must also be explained in words, so that the reader can understand how the mathematical model represents the real decision problem.

Implementation in Python
The optimization model must be implemented in Python. The implementation must be consistent with the mathematical formulation presented in the report. Your code should be clear, organized, and sufficiently documented so that another person can understand it and reproduce the main results.

Analysis of results
Running the model once and reporting the optimal objective value is not enough. The purpose of the model is to obtain useful insights about the decision problem. You should therefore perform an appropriate sensitivity analysis.

Your analysis should address questions such as what the model recommend, why this recommendation is useful, or how the results change when important parameters or assumptions change.

Final report
There is no mandatory report template. Choosing how to organize and communicate your work is part of the project. Write the report as if it were being delivered to a decision-maker who understands the application area but is not an Operations Research expert.

The reader should be able to clearly understand:

What is the problem and why does it matter?

How was it modeled?

What does the model recommend?

What did you learn from the analysis?

The report must include the complete mathematical formulation and maintain the expected mathematical rigor.

The final submission must include the final report, python code and data needed to reproduce the main results.

Use of Generative AI
The use of Generative AI is allowed and encouraged as part of the learning process, according to the course policy. You may use AI tools to support activities such as brainstorming, mathematical modeling, Python coding, debugging, analysis, or writing.

However, you are responsible for everything you submit. You must understand and be able to explain your mathematical formulation, Python code, assumptions, modeling choices, and results. AI-generated outputs must be checked critically and should not be accepted without verification.

Final exam
Your individual understanding of the project will be assessed in the final written exam.

The exam will include one or more questions about your own project. For example: explain one important constraint, or explain one important assumption and its consequences.

The objective is not to determine which tools you used to complete the project. The objective is to ensure that each student understands and can justify the work submitted by the team.
